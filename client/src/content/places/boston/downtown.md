Downtown Boston covers Downtown Crossing, Boston Common and the blocks around Faneuil Hall and Government Center. Offices, shops and the State House are all close together here, so it suits a lunch with coworkers, drinks after work, or a day out with friends who ride in on different lines.

Spots that are easy to name in a plan:

- the Park Street station entrance at the corner of Boston Common
- the pedestrian streets of Downtown Crossing, around Washington and Winter Streets
- Quincy Market at Faneuil Hall Marketplace
- City Hall Plaza, next to the Government Center station

Several stations sit a few blocks apart, and each has a different mix of lines. Pick the spot by the line most of the group rides.

## Getting around

- **[Park Street](https://www.mbta.com/stops/place-pktrm)** has the Red Line and the Green Line's B, C, D and E branches. **Boylston**, the next Green Line stop south, has the same four branches.
- **[Downtown Crossing](https://www.mbta.com/stops/place-dwnxg)** has the Red and Orange lines.
- **State** has the Orange and Blue lines.
- **Government Center** has the Blue Line and all four Green Line branches.
- **Haymarket**, near Faneuil Hall, has the Orange Line and the Green Line's D and E branches.

The subway closes overnight. The MBTA's [subway guide](https://www.mbta.com/guides/subway-guide) gives typical hours of 5 a.m. to 1 a.m. Monday to Thursday, 5 a.m. to 2 a.m. on Friday and Saturday, and 6 a.m. to 1 a.m. on Sunday.

Park Street, Downtown Crossing, State, Government Center and Haymarket are all on the guide's list of stations for the last connections of the night. Anyone changing lines on the way home should be at one of them by 12:35 a.m. Sunday to Thursday, or by 1:30 a.m. on Friday and Saturday.
