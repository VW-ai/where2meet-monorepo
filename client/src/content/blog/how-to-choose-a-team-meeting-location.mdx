Your team works from different places. Some people live downtown, some live past the last subway stop, and a few work from home most of the week. When it's time to meet in person, "let's just use the office" isn't always the easiest answer. And "somewhere central" usually means central for whoever suggested it.

This guide covers how to pick a team meeting location everyone can reach without a long trip, how to choose the right kind of venue, and how to settle on a place without a week of back-and-forth.

## Start with where people are coming from

List where each person will travel from on the day of the meeting. That isn't always home. Someone might come from a client site, a school drop-off or another office.

Then note how each person will get there. A spot that's 15 minutes away by car can be 45 minutes by transit. In dense neighborhoods with little parking, it can be the other way around. If half the team takes the train, a place next to a station matters more than a place with a big parking lot.

## Compare travel time, not distance

Distance on a map is misleading. A venue 5 miles away on a direct train line can be quicker to reach than one 2 miles away across a river. What matters is how long each person spends getting there.

Look at two numbers for every option:

- **The longest trip.** If one person faces an hour each way, that place probably isn't the one, even when everyone else is close.
- **The spread.** A place where trips take 24, 27 and 30 minutes usually works better than one where they take 12, 15 and 52.

Time of day matters too. A drive that takes 20 minutes at 10 a.m. can take 40 at 5 p.m. Schedule the meeting outside rush hour if you can, and check routes for the hour people will actually travel.

## Pick the right kind of place

The best location depends on what the meeting is for.

- **Working sessions** need a quiet table, Wi-Fi and power outlets. A coworking space with day passes or a bookable meeting room often beats a cafe.
- **Planning or sensitive conversations** need privacy. A library study room or a restaurant's private room keeps the discussion out of earshot.
- **Team lunches and check-ins** are fine at a cafe or restaurant, as long as it takes reservations for your group and isn't too loud to talk.

Before you commit, check a few things:

- Can you reserve a table or room for your group size?
- Can everyone get in, including step-free access if anyone needs it?
- How loud is it at the time you'll be there?
- Does it fit the budget, including parking or transit fares?

## Decide without the group chat spiral

Picking a place in a group chat tends to stall. Someone suggests a spot, two people react, and nobody commits. A few habits help.

1. **Shortlist two or three options** that pass the travel-time check.
2. **Ask everyone to vote** instead of asking an open question like "anywhere good for you?"
3. **Give one person the final call.** The organizer picks from the top votes.
4. **Send everything in one message.** Include the address, a map link and the start time, so nobody has to scroll back for it.

## How to do it in Where2Meet

Where2Meet is a free web app for planning where to meet as a group. Nobody needs an account.

1. Open [where2meet.org](/), choose **Team meeting**, pick a date and time, and add your name.
2. Share the meeting link with your team. Each person adds where they're coming from. Anyone who'd rather not share an exact address can show an approximate area instead. You can also add everyone's starting points yourself.
3. Search for places around the group, such as cafes or coworking spaces, or look up a specific place by name.
4. Select a place to see each person's route and travel time on the map. Switch between car, transit, walking and bike to match how people will travel.
5. Everyone votes for the places that work for them, and the organizer picks the final spot.

Changes show up for everyone on the link without a refresh.

## A quick checklist

- You know everyone's starting point and how they'll travel.
- The longest trip is reasonable, and the trips are close to each other in length.
- The meeting avoids rush hour, or you checked routes for that hour.
- The venue suits the meeting. It's quiet enough, bookable, accessible and within budget.
- Two or three options went to a vote, and one person made the final call.
- Everyone has the address, a map link and the start time.
