"We should hang out this weekend" is easy to say. Turning it into a plan is harder when your friends live in different parts of town. Someone suggests a place near them, someone else says it's too far, and by Saturday afternoon nobody has decided anything.

This guide walks through picking what to do, finding a spot everyone can get to, and locking in the plan before the weekend slips away.

## Decide what you're doing first

The activity narrows the map, so choose it before the place. A few common options:

- **Low-key.** Coffee, a walk, or a park with room to sit. People can join late or leave early.
- **Food.** A food hall, a brunch spot or a casual restaurant. Food halls work well for groups, because everyone can order what they want and pay separately.
- **Something to do.** Bowling, mini golf, a climbing gym, a museum or a board game cafe. These give the group a shared focus, but many need a booking.
- **Evening.** A bar with games or a spot with live music. Check whether it takes reservations for groups.

If the group can't agree on an activity, offer two and let people vote. That's faster than an open "what does everyone feel like?"

## Plan around weekend travel

Getting around on a weekend isn't the same as on a weekday. In many cities, buses and trains run less often on Saturdays and Sundays, and weekend track work can close a line for the day. Parking near popular areas can also fill up by early afternoon.

So check routes for the day and time people will actually travel. For example, a place that's 20 minutes away on a Tuesday can take 35 on a Sunday if the train comes every 20 minutes instead of every 8.

## Find a spot everyone can reach

Compare how long each person will spend getting there, not how far the place looks on a map. Two numbers help:

- **The longest trip.** If one friend faces an hour each way, they may quietly skip it.
- **How close the trips are.** Trips of 20, 25 and 30 minutes are easier to agree on than 5, 10 and 50.

When some friends drive and others take transit, look for places near a station that also have parking nearby. A neighborhood with several options within walking distance helps too, because the group can switch spots without anyone traveling again.

## Pick a place that works for a group

A great spot for two people can be a bad one for six. Before you send it to the group, check a few things:

- Can you book a table or a lane for your group size, or do they only take walk-ins?
- Can people arrive at different times without holding everyone else up?
- Is there a backup nearby if it's full, or if it rains?
- Does it fit everyone's budget?

Parks and food halls handle most of these well. Popular brunch spots with long waits often don't.

## Lock it in before the weekend

Weekend plans fall apart when the decision drags past Friday. A few habits help.

1. **Share two or three options** with the travel times for each.
2. **Set a deadline for votes,** such as Thursday evening.
3. **Let one person make the final call** from the top votes.
4. **Send one message with everything.** Include the address, a map link, the time and the backup spot.

## How to do it in Where2Meet

Where2Meet is a free web app for planning where to meet as a group. Nobody needs an account.

1. Open [where2meet.org](/), choose **Weekend hangout**, pick a day and time, and add your name.
2. Share the link in your group chat. Each friend adds where they're coming from. Anyone can show an approximate area instead of an exact address. You can also add everyone's starting points yourself.
3. Search around the group for what you want to do, such as a food hall, a park or a bowling alley, or look up a specific place by name.
4. Select a place to see each friend's route and travel time. Switch between car, transit, walking and bike to match how people will get there.
5. Everyone votes for the places they'd go to, and the organizer picks the final spot.

Changes show up for everyone on the link without a refresh.

## A quick checklist

- The group picked an activity first.
- You checked routes for the day and time people will travel, not a weekday.
- The longest trip is reasonable, and the trips are close to each other in length.
- The place can take your group, or it's easy to walk in.
- There's a backup nearby if it's full or it rains.
- Votes closed before the weekend, and one person made the call.
- Everyone has the address, a map link, the time and the backup.
