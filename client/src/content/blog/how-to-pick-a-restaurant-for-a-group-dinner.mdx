Planning a group dinner sounds simple until you try it. A group of friends needs a table on the same night, in a part of town everyone can reach, at a place with something everyone can eat.

This guide covers how to find a restaurant that takes big groups, how to get everyone there before the table is given away, and how to settle the menu, the bill and the headcount before the night.

## Find a restaurant for a big group

Not every restaurant can seat a large group, and the ones that can often have rules for it. Check these before you share a place with the group:

- **Group bookings.** Many restaurants take online bookings only up to a set party size, often six or eight. For more, you may need to call or book a private room.
- **Set menus.** Some restaurants ask large groups to order a set or family-style menu. That makes ordering easier, but check the price per person first.
- **Deposits and fees.** Some take a card to hold a large booking and charge for no-shows or late cancellations. Some also add a service charge to the bill for groups.
- **The table itself.** Ask whether the group sits at one table or two. A round table or a large booth keeps everyone in one conversation better than a long row.

Food made for sharing, such as Korean barbecue, dim sum or family-style Italian, suits a group well. The dishes land in the middle of the table, and nobody waits for one plate that's running late.

## Get everyone there before the table goes

A group booking comes with a clock. Some restaurants hold a table for only 15 minutes, and many seat a group only when most of it is there. One person on a slow trip can cost the whole group its table.

So compare arrival times, not just distance. For example, if five friends leave work at 7:00 for a 7:30 table, a place 55 minutes from one of them gets that friend there at 7:55, after a 15-minute hold runs out. A place by a busier station gets all five there by 7:30.

## Settle the menu and the bill early

Food and money cause most of the friction at a group dinner, so sort them out before anyone sits down.

- Ask about dietary needs when you share the options, and check each restaurant's menu online before you book.
- Agree on how you'll split the bill. Some places won't split a check more than a few ways, so ask when you book.
- If the restaurant asks for a set menu, share the price per person with the group before you confirm.

## Confirm the headcount

Group numbers change. Someone joins late, and someone else drops out the day before. Restaurants plan their tables and staff around your number, and some charge for empty seats.

1. **Set a reply date** a few days before the dinner.
2. **Confirm the final number** with the restaurant the day before.
3. **Call ahead** if someone will be late, so the restaurant keeps the table.
4. **Send everyone one message.** Include the address, a map link, the time and the name the booking is under.

## How to do it in Where2Meet

Where2Meet is a free web app for planning where to meet as a group. Nobody needs an account.

1. Open [where2meet.org](/), choose **Group dinner**, pick a date and time, and add your name.
2. Share the link with the group. Each person adds where they're coming from, such as work or home. Anyone can show an approximate area instead of an exact address. You can also add everyone's starting points yourself.
3. Search for restaurants around the group, or look up a specific restaurant by name.
4. Select a restaurant to see each person's route and travel time. Switch between car, transit, walking and bike to match how people will get there.
5. Everyone votes for the places they'd go to, and the organizer picks the final spot.

Changes show up for everyone on the link without a refresh.

## A quick checklist

- The restaurant takes bookings for your group size, and you know its rules for groups.
- Everyone can arrive before the table's hold runs out, at the hour they'll travel.
- The menu works for everyone's dietary needs.
- You agreed how to split the bill.
- The restaurant has your final headcount.
- Everyone has the address, a map link, the time and the booking name.
