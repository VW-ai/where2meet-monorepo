"Where should we go?" is a harder question than it sounds on a date. You want a place that feels right, but you live in different neighborhoods, one of you may be coming from work, and nobody wants to spend half the evening on a train.

This guide covers how to pick a date spot you can both reach easily, how to plan the trip home as carefully as the trip there, and how to settle on a place without a long back-and-forth.

## Start with where you're both coming from

On a weeknight, one or both of you may be coming straight from work. On a weekend, it's probably home. Ask, because the answer changes which neighborhoods make sense.

Then think about how each of you will travel. If one person drives and the other takes the train, a place near a station with parking close by saves you both a headache. If you're both on transit, a spot one stop from a transfer point usually beats one deep in a neighborhood only one of you knows.

## Plan the trip home, too

Most people plan how to get to a date and forget about getting back. Late at night, trains and buses run less often, the last train can leave earlier than you expect, and rides can cost more.

Check the route home for the hour you'll actually leave, not the hour you arrive. For example, take a bar that's a 5-minute walk from Ben's door. At 7 p.m., Ana gets there in 35 minutes. At 11 p.m., her train comes every 30 minutes instead of every 10, so her trip home takes 55. A bar by a busier station takes Ana 20 minutes to get there and 25 to get home, and Ben 20 minutes by bike either way.

## Pick a place that fits the date

- **First dates** go well somewhere you can talk and leave easily, such as a coffee bar, a wine bar with counter seats or a casual restaurant. Skip places so loud you have to shout.
- **Later dates** can be a longer dinner, a show or an activity like bowling or a cooking class. These usually need a booking.
- **Anniversaries and special nights** are worth a reservation at a place with a quiet table.

Before you suggest a place, check a few things:

- Does it take reservations, or will you wait at the door?
- How loud is it at the hour you'll be there?
- Does the menu work for both of you, including any dietary needs?
- Does the price suit the kind of night you have in mind?

Even a good plan can fall through. The kitchen closes early, the wait is an hour, or the music is louder than the reviews said. So pick a neighborhood with a second option a short walk away. You can move without anyone getting back on a train, and if the date is going well, you have somewhere to go next.

## Settle it without the back-and-forth

A long "where should we go?" thread can drain the excitement before the date starts. A few habits help.

1. **Suggest two options** instead of asking an open question.
2. **Say how long each takes** for both of you, there and back.
3. **Let your date choose**, or agree that whoever plans this one decides.
4. **Send everything in one message.** Include the address, a map link, the time and the backup spot.

## How to do it in Where2Meet

Where2Meet is a free web app for planning where to meet. Nobody needs an account.

1. Open [where2meet.org](/), choose **Date night**, pick a day and time, and add your name.
2. Send the link to your date. Each of you adds where you're coming from. If you'd rather not share an exact address yet, you can show an approximate area instead.
3. Search for places around both of you, such as wine bars or restaurants, or look up a specific place by name.
4. Select a place to see each person's route and travel time. Switch between car, transit, walking and bike to match how you'll each get there.
5. You both vote for the places you like, and the person who made the plan picks the final spot.

Changes show up for both of you without a refresh.

## A quick checklist

- You know where you're each coming from and how you'll travel.
- You checked the trip home for the hour you'll leave.
- The place fits the date. You can talk there, it takes your booking, and the menu works for both of you.
- There's a backup a short walk away.
- You suggested two options instead of asking an open question.
- You both have the address, a map link, the time and the backup.
