The best places to hang out in Downtown Boston on a weekend sit a few minutes from a T stop and stay open from Saturday afternoon into the night. Pick one cluster, Temple Place near Park Street or State Street near the waterfront, and the group moves between spots on foot. You're getting friends together on a Saturday or Sunday. Some ride the Red Line, some the Orange or Green, and nobody wants to spend the afternoon changing trains.

This post suggests seven spots, then covers a sample Saturday, which cluster to pick and how late the group can stay. It's part of our guide to [Downtown Boston](/blog/boston/downtown).

## Which places to hang out in Downtown Boston suit a weekend?

Choose by start time. Two bars open at 10 a.m. on weekends, the rest open between 11:30 a.m. and 5 p.m., and six of the seven stay open past midnight on Saturday.

The hours, reservations, group fit, price levels and walk times on these cards come from Google Maps as of November 2026. Walk times start at the nearest MBTA station, or at the next pick.

## What does a Saturday look like from start to finish?

Start where the doors open first, then move once or twice, not every hour. Here is an example plan for the Temple Place cluster.

1. **11:30 a.m.** Lunch at Back Deck.
2. **3 p.m.** Walk about 6 minutes to VERSUS as it opens for a round of games.
3. **5 p.m.** Walk about 5 minutes to Hobgoblin for dinner.
4. **Late.** Cross Temple Place to The Wig Shop.

Sunday runs shorter. Back Deck closes at 8 p.m., VERSUS at 11 p.m. and Hobgoblin at midnight, so The Wig Shop is the one late stop in that cluster.

## Which part of downtown should the group pick?

Pick the cluster by the line most of the group rides. The two clusters are about 10 to 14 minutes apart on foot.

- **Temple Place and Downtown Crossing.** Back Deck, VERSUS, Hobgoblin and The Wig Shop. [Downtown Crossing](https://www.mbta.com/stops/place-dwnxg) has the Red and Orange lines, and [Park Street](https://www.mbta.com/stops/place-pktrm) has the Red Line and all four Green Line branches.
- **State Street.** The Black Rose and Apex sit next door to each other, under a minute apart. [Aquarium](https://www.mbta.com/stops/place-aqucl) on the Blue Line is about 3 to 4 minutes away, and [State](https://www.mbta.com/stops/place-state), with the Orange and Blue lines, about 5.
- **Hanover Street.** The Point is about 3 minutes from [Haymarket](https://www.mbta.com/stops/place-haecl), which has the Orange Line and the Green Line's D and E branches. Apex is about 7 minutes away on foot.

On a dry day, the free open spaces fill the gap before the 3 p.m. openings. Boston Common's Frog Pond is about a 6-minute walk from Hobgoblin, and the Rose Kennedy Greenway is a short walk from The Point.

## How late can the group stay out?

Later than the subway runs. Most picks stay open until 2 a.m. on Saturday, but the MBTA's [subway guide](https://www.mbta.com/guides/subway-guide) says riders who change lines should reach a downtown transfer station by 1:30 a.m. on Friday and Saturday, or 12:35 a.m. Sunday to Thursday.

Park Street, Downtown Crossing, State and Haymarket are all on that list.

## Common questions

### What's open early on a weekend in Downtown Boston?

The Black Rose and The Point open at 10 a.m. on Saturday and Sunday. Back Deck opens at 11:30 a.m. every day.

### Is there something to do besides eat and drink?

VERSUS is a video arcade on Province Street, about a 3-minute walk from Downtown Crossing. It opens at 3 p.m. on Saturday and Sunday.

### Do we need a reservation on a Saturday?

Five picks take reservations: Back Deck, The Point, Apex, Hobgoblin and The Wig Shop. We couldn't confirm reservations at the Black Rose or VERSUS, so a big group should arrive early there.
