The easiest places to hang out in Downtown Boston on a Saturday night are pubs that take no reservations and stay open until 1:30 or 2 a.m. Pick the cluster nearest the group's T line, walk in early, and leave by about 1:15 a.m. if anyone needs a train. Nobody knows if six friends or eleven will show up, so nobody wants to book, and everyone wants to know how they'll get home.

This post lists seven walk-in spots in three clusters, then covers getting a group in, picking a cluster and the last trains. It's part of our guide to [Downtown Boston](/blog/boston/downtown).

## Which places to hang out downtown take walk-ins on a Saturday night?

All seven take no reservations, and all stay open until at least 1:30 a.m. on Saturday.

## How does a group get in without a booking?

Arrive early and stay close to a second option, since a bar with no reservations can't hold a table.

1. **Go before 9 p.m. with seven or more.** Later arrivals join the people already inside.
2. **Send one or two people ahead.** They claim space and text the rest where to find them.
3. **Pick a cluster, not one bar.** Bell in Hand, The White Bull and The Point are about 2 minutes apart. If one is full, walk to the next.
4. **Bring ID.** The drinking age in Massachusetts is 21.

## Which cluster should the group pick?

Pick the one on the line most people ride home, since the late trip is the harder one. The clusters are about 5 to 10 minutes apart on foot.

- **Union and Hanover streets.** Bell in Hand, The White Bull and The Point. [Haymarket](https://www.mbta.com/stops/place-haecl) has the Orange Line and the Green Line's D and E branches, and [State](https://www.mbta.com/stops/place-state) the Orange and Blue lines.
- **Chatham and Milk streets.** The Hong Kong and Coogan's. [Aquarium](https://www.mbta.com/stops/place-aqucl) is the Blue Line stop, and State is about 4 to 5 minutes away.
- **Downtown Crossing and Boston Common.** The Hub Pub and El Jefe's. [Downtown Crossing](https://www.mbta.com/stops/place-dwnxg) has the Red and Orange lines, and [Park Street](https://www.mbta.com/stops/place-pktrm) the Red Line and all four Green Line branches.

## How late can the group stay out and still take the T home?

Until about 1:15 a.m. on a Saturday. The MBTA's [subway guide](https://www.mbta.com/guides/subway-guide) says that to make the final connections, riders should reach a downtown transfer station by 1:30 a.m. on Friday or Saturday night, or by 12:35 a.m. Sunday to Thursday.

- **The transfer stations.** The guide lists Downtown Crossing, Government Center, Haymarket, North Station, Park Street and State. Boylston isn't one, so walk from El Jefe's to Park Street.
- **The Commuter Rail.** It keeps its own timetable, so riders should check their line's [schedule](https://www.mbta.com/schedules/commuter-rail) first.
- **Weekend changes.** Check the MBTA's [subway alerts](https://www.mbta.com/alerts/subway) on Saturday afternoon.

Anyone staying until 2 a.m. needs a taxi or a ride-hail, so agree on that before the first round.

## Common questions

### Where can we eat after midnight downtown?

El Jefe's Taqueria on Boylston Street serves until 2 a.m. every day.

### What time does the T stop running on Saturday night?

Service runs until about 2 a.m. on Friday and Saturday. To change lines, reach a downtown transfer station by 1:30 a.m.

### Does this plan work on a Sunday?

Partly. Coogan's is closed, The Point closes at midnight, and the transfer cutoff is 12:35 a.m. Start earlier.
