A good graduation party venue in Boston is one you can book for the right hour, close to the station your guests already use. For a morning ceremony that means a reserved lunch, and for a big family it means a room of your own. Grandparents are flying into Logan, the graduate's friends live in Somerville, and everyone wants to celebrate the same afternoon.

This post lists eight places, then covers lunch or dinner, when to book a private room, and which stations suit which guests. It's part of our guide to [Boston](/blog/boston).

## Which graduation party venue in Boston fits your group?

Match the place to the meal and the head count. Five are restaurants that take reservations, and three are event spaces you book by date.

## Should the party be lunch after the ceremony or dinner that night?

Choose lunch when the ceremony ends before noon and relatives drive home the same day. Choose dinner when the graduate wants friends there too.

On weekends, Contessa, Rochambeau, 75 on Liberty Wharf and MIDA all serve before 11 a.m. That gives you a table soon after a morning ceremony. Book for the time the ceremony ends plus the trip over, not for the time it ends. Gowns, photos and finding parents in a crowd all take longer than planned.

A dinner gives you more room to choose. Contessa, MIDA and 75 on Liberty Wharf serve until at least 10 p.m. every night, so a 6 p.m. booking leaves time for toasts.

## When does a graduation party need a private room?

Book an event space once the guest list is longer than one table. Ask each venue for its capacity and minimum spend before you send a single invitation.

- **Hampshire House** suits a family party on the Beacon Hill side of the Public Garden.
- **The State Room** suits a downtown reception a short walk from the Orange and Blue lines.
- **Upstairs at Bow** suits a party for friends who live north of the river.

These three book by date. Settle the venue first, then set the party's day around what it has open. If the guest list fits a big table after all, see our [restaurant for a big group in Boston](/blog/boston/restaurant-big-group-boston).

## Which station works for relatives and friends?

Start from where the oldest guests arrive. They're the ones who should transfer least.

- **From Logan Airport.** The Blue Line runs from Airport station to State. The Silver Line SL1 runs from the terminals to World Trade Center and South Station.
- **From the suburbs.** Commuter rail trains stop at South Station, Back Bay and North Station.
- **From Cambridge and Somerville.** The Red Line reaches Downtown Crossing, and the Green Line D branch runs from Union Square.

If the graduates head out after dinner, check the MBTA's [subway guide](https://www.mbta.com/guides/subway-guide). It says to reach a downtown transfer station by 1:30 a.m. on Friday and Saturday to make the last connections.

## Common questions

### How far ahead should you book a graduation party venue in Boston?

Book as soon as the ceremony date is set. Other graduates' families want the same weekend, and event spaces book by date.

### Is there a moderately priced place for a graduation dinner in Boston?

MIDA and 75 on Liberty Wharf are both moderately priced and take reservations. Rochambeau is expensive, and Contessa is very expensive.

### Where can graduates go after the family dinner?

Fete is the late pick in this list, a short walk from the Red and Orange lines. Plan the trip home around the last T connections.
