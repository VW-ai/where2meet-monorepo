The best group study spots in Back Bay are the Boston Public Library's bookable spaces at Copley, plus a few cafes for the part of the session when you talk out loud. Book the library first if you want a set two hours, and move to Trident Booksellers & Cafe when it closes. You have a midterm coming, classmates on two T lines, and no table big enough at the first cafe you try.

This post, part of our [Back Bay](/blog/boston/back-bay) guide, lists seven places, explains library bookings by head count, and matches each pick to its T stop. For downtown picks, see [study group meeting place ideas in Boston](/blog/boston/study-group-meeting-place-boston).

## Which group study spots in Back Bay fit your group?

Choose by head count and how long you need the space. Book a room for a long block of work, and walk in to a cafe for a shorter review.

## How do you book a study room at the Boston Public Library?

Pick the space by head count, because the library splits its bookings at six people. Neither booking page lists a fee, so confirm the cost when you apply.

1. **Up to 6 people.** Book a [KBLIC alcove](https://bpl.libcal.com/reserve/meetingroom) in the Kirstein Business Library, on the lower level. Each group gets 2 hours a day and 3 sessions a week. Check in at the Info Desk when you arrive.
2. **7 to 20 people.** Apply for a [mezzanine study room](https://www.bpl.org/study-room-application/) at least 2 business days ahead and no more than 30 days out. You get 2 hours and one booking a week. The room isn't yours until the library's Special Events Department approves the request.
3. **Meet in person.** Study rooms have no video conferencing and aren't meant for virtual meetings.
4. **Bring only covered drinks.** Food isn't allowed in the library, so eat at Newsfeed Café first.

The library's [community rooms](https://www.bpl.org/reservable-community-spaces/) are for nonprofits holding free public meetings, so skip that form.

## Which T stop should each person use?

Copley is closest to the library and Capital One Café, and Arlington is closest to Workbar and Life Alive. Trident is about 12 minutes on foot from Copley but 2 from Hynes.

- **Green Line B, C and D riders.** All three stop at Arlington, Copley and Hynes.
- **Green Line E riders.** The E skips Hynes. Prudential is about a 10-minute walk from Trident.
- **Orange Line and commuter rail riders.** Back Bay station is about a 4-minute walk from the library, but about 17 minutes from Trident, so favor the Copley picks.
- **Classmates who need an elevator.** [Hynes has no elevators](https://www.mbta.com/stops/place-hymnl). Prudential, Copley, Arlington and Back Bay all have step-free access.

From November 7 to 15, 2026, the MBTA's [Green Line alerts](https://www.mbta.com/schedules/Green/alerts) list no trains through Arlington, Copley or Hynes. Riders take shuttle buses to Copley and Back Bay, or the Orange Line from North Station to Back Bay. That week, start from a pick near Back Bay station.

## Common questions

### Can you reserve a table to study at a Back Bay cafe?

Trident takes reservations. Tatte, Life Alive and Capital One Café don't, so arrive early. Capital One's [Back Bay page](https://www.capitalone.com/local/boston-backbay/) says account holders can reserve a private nook, so ask whether this cafe has one.

### Where can a study group keep working after the library closes?

Trident stays open to 10 p.m. every day and Life Alive to 9 p.m. Tatte closes at 8 p.m., or 7 p.m. on Sunday.

### Where can a study group meet on a Sunday morning in Back Bay?

Start at a cafe, because the library doesn't open until 11 a.m. on Sunday. Life Alive opens at 7 a.m., Tatte at 7:30 a.m., and Trident and Capital One Café at 8 a.m.
