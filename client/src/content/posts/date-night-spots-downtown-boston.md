The best date night spots in Downtown Boston are a few minutes on foot from Park Street or Downtown Crossing, where the Red, Green and Orange lines meet. Temple Place and Winter Place, two short streets off Washington Street, hold three of the picks below. You and your date may ride different lines, and one of you has to suggest a place that is easy for both and easy to leave.

This post suggests seven places, from a rooftop bar to a walk on Boston Common. Then it covers which station to meet at, how to plan two stops in one night, and when to leave for the last train. It's part of our guide to [Downtown Boston](/blog/boston/downtown), and [date spot ideas in Boston near the T](/blog/boston/date-spot-ideas-boston) covers the rest of the city.

## Which date night spots in Downtown Boston should you pick?

Pick by where each of you starts. Five of the seven are within about a 4-minute walk of Downtown Crossing, and the two bars farther out suit a date who arrives at State or South Station.

Plan around the closed nights. On a Monday, Apex, Fete and OFFSUIT are all closed, so Yvonne's, Estella and Hobgoblin are your indoor choices. On a Sunday, only OFFSUIT is closed.

## Which T stop should you meet at?

Meet at Downtown Crossing if one of you rides the Red Line and the other the Orange, since both lines stop there. A Green Line rider gets off at Park Street, the next Red Line stop.

Name a corner rather than a station. Tremont Street at Temple Place, across the street from Boston Common, is a short walk from Park Street, and Estella and Hobgoblin are on Temple Place itself.

A Blue Line rider has a shorter trip to Apex, near State station, where the Orange Line also stops. Someone coming in by commuter rail or the Red Line from the south can start at OFFSUIT, near South Station.

## How do you plan a date night with two stops?

Keep both stops on foot and agree on the first one. Estella and Hobgoblin are about 30 meters apart on Temple Place, so a drink at one can turn into dinner at the other.

Yvonne's is about a 3-minute walk from both. It's in the Winter Place building that held [Locke-Ober](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locke-Ober), a Boston restaurant that closed in 2012.

![](locke-ober-building-winter-place-downtown-boston.jpg)

A walk on Boston Common works as the first stop or the last. In winter, the Frog Pond in the middle of the Common is a skating rink, as the [City of Boston's page for the Common](https://www.boston.gov/parks/boston-common) says. Check the rink's hours on the day before you plan around it.

## How late can you stay and still catch the T?

Be at Park Street, Downtown Crossing, State or Government Center by 12:35 a.m. Sunday to Thursday, or 1:30 a.m. on Friday and Saturday. The MBTA's [subway guide](https://www.mbta.com/guides/subway-guide) gives those times for the last connections between lines.

Fete, Estella, Hobgoblin, Yvonne's and Apex all stay open until 2 a.m. on Friday and Saturday, so the bar won't end the night before the trains do. Boston Common closes at 11:30 p.m., so walk there first if it's part of the plan.

Commuter rail runs on its own timetable. If one of you rides it home from South Station, check the [MBTA commuter rail schedules](https://www.mbta.com/schedules/commuter-rail) before you pick a late stop.

## Common questions

### Which Downtown Boston date spots are open on a Monday?

Yvonne's, Estella and Hobgoblin all open on Mondays, along with Boston Common. Apex, Fete and OFFSUIT are closed that night.

### Is there a free date night idea in Downtown Boston?

Yes. A walk on Boston Common costs nothing, and it's at the Park Street station. The park closes at 11:30 p.m.

### How do I get to Downtown Crossing from Cambridge?

Take the Red Line toward Ashmont or Braintree from Harvard, Central or Kendall/MIT. Downtown Crossing is the stop after Park Street, so you don't have to change trains.
