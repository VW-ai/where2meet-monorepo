Pickup basketball in New York is easiest at an outdoor court on a train your whole group already rides. Rucker Park in Harlem and the West 4th Street courts in Greenwich Village are the famous ones, and trains from the Bronx and Brooklyn stop near both. Your friends live all over the city, and every week someone spends an hour getting to the game.

This post sorts seven courts by the trains that reach them without a transfer. Then it covers how many players you need, a backup for rain and the trains home. It is part of our guide to [New York](/blog/new-york).

## Where to play pickup basketball close to everyone's train?

Pick the court your group reaches without a transfer.

:::places

The hours and walk times on these cards come from Google Maps as of October 2026, except Pier 2's hours, which Brooklyn Bridge Park sets. The lines come from MTA schedules.

### Uptown, on the B, C and D

The D stops at 155 St, near Rucker Park, at all hours, so players from the Bronx and Brooklyn ride straight there. The C stops at the other 155 St station, on St. Nicholas Avenue, about an 8-minute walk away.

### Downtown, on nine lines

West 4th has the most lines of these picks, so riders from Queens, Brooklyn and the Bronx get there without a transfer. Grand Street sits on the B and D, Seward Park on the F, Tompkins Square on the L and Pier 25 on the 1.

![](lower-east-side-playground-basketball-courts-east-village.jpg)

### Brooklyn, on the A, C, 2 and 3

Pier 2 in Brooklyn Bridge Park suits players from Brooklyn and Lower Manhattan, and the A and the 2 reach it from Queens and the Bronx. The park's [basketball page](https://www.brooklynbridgepark.org/attractions/basketball/) lists lockers and restrooms on the pier.

## How many players do you need for a pickup game?

A full-court game needs ten, five on each side. Half court needs six for three on three, or four for two on two. Ask for a yes or a no a day ahead, and count the yeses before you pick the court.

## What's the backup if it rains or the court is full?

Pick a second court on the same line, so nobody has to transfer. The D reaches Rucker, West 4th and Grand Street, the F reaches West 4th and Seward Park, and the A and C reach West 4th and Pier 2.

![](tompkins-square-park-snow-east-village.jpg)

For rain, set a cutoff time, such as two hours before the game, to call it off or move it. Indoors, NYC Parks gyms are free for players under 25, as in our list of [Manhattan's indoor Parks gyms](/blog/new-york/basketball-courts-near-me-free-indoor-nyc).

## How does everyone get home after a late game?

Pick a court on a line that runs all night. The A, D, E, F, L, 1 and 2 do, but the B, C, M and 3 don't. Grand Street is open until 1 a.m. and Pier 25 until midnight.

![](pier-25-hudson-river-park-tribeca-skyline.jpg)

After a late game at Rucker, take the D. From Pier 2, walk to High St for the A or to Clark St for the 2. The MTA's [late-night subway map](https://www.mta.info/map/5336) shows every overnight line, and its [planned service changes](https://www.mta.info/alerts) show weekend track work.

For food after the game, see [how to pick a restaurant for a group dinner](/blog/new-york/lower-manhattan/how-to-pick-a-restaurant-for-a-group-dinner).

## Common questions

### How do I find basketball courts near me in New York?

NYC Parks lists its outdoor courts by borough and ZIP code on its [basketball courts page](https://www.nycgovparks.org/facilities/basketball). Google Maps shows them too.

### Do you need to reserve a basketball court in New York?

Not for pickup basketball. NYC Parks asks organized leagues and special events to get a permit, and a pickup game just shows up and plays. Brooklyn Bridge Park lets groups reserve the Pier 2 courts by permit, so one may be booked when you arrive.

### Is it free to play at outdoor courts in New York?

Yes. The courts in this post are in public parks, with no fee to play. Indoor courts at NYC Parks recreation centers need a [membership](https://www.nycgovparks.org/facilities/recreationcenters).
