The best study group meeting place in Boston is usually the Central Library at Copley or a cafe a few minutes from a downtown T station. Choose the library when the group needs to work quietly, and a cafe when you need to talk problems through. Your study group has people in Cambridge, Roxbury and Allston, an exam on Thursday, and no room on campus that everyone can reach.

This post lists seven picks near the T. Then it covers how to mix quiet work with talking, how late each place stays open, and which stations suit which riders. It's part of our guide to [Boston](/blog/boston).

## Which study group meeting place in Boston fits your group?

Decide how much your group will talk. Silent reading fits the library, and working through problem sets out loud fits a cafe.

Walk times are Google Maps walking directions from the station each note names.

Three picks are on Boylston Street at Copley, in [Back Bay](/blog/boston/back-bay). Copley station has every Green Line branch, so Green Line riders get there without a transfer.

## How do you mix quiet study and group discussion?

Split the session between two places on the same block. Work alone in the reading room, then move to a cafe table to compare answers.

At Copley, that means an hour or two in Bates Hall, then Newsfeed Café or Capital One Café when you need to talk. Agree on the switch time before you start, so nobody sits waiting.

A private study group usually can't book one of the library's free community rooms. Its [reservable spaces page](https://www.bpl.org/reservable-community-spaces/) gives them to nonprofits holding free meetings open to the public.

## How late can a study group stay?

Trident stays open the latest, until 10 p.m. every night. For a session after dinner, start there or at Caffè Nero.

- **Until 10 p.m.** Trident, every day.
- **Until 9 p.m.** Caffè Nero, Monday to Saturday. It closes at 8 p.m. on Sunday.
- **Until 8 p.m.** BON! every day, and the library Monday to Thursday. The library closes at 5 p.m. Friday to Sunday.
- **Earlier.** Capital One Café closes at 7 p.m. on weekdays, Newsfeed Café at 5 p.m. and Ogawa Coffee at 4 p.m.

On Sunday, the library opens at 11 a.m., so plan a morning session at a cafe. The MBTA's [subway guide](https://www.mbta.com/guides/subway-guide) lists when the last trains leave.

## Which T stations work for students across Boston?

Match the place to the line most of the group rides. Copley and Hynes are on the Green Line, and Park Street adds the Red Line.

Students from Cambridge on the Red Line can get off at Park Street for BON!, or at Downtown Crossing for Caffè Nero. Orange Line riders can get off at Chinatown for Caffè Nero or at State for Ogawa Coffee. Green Line riders reach Copley and Hynes without a transfer, though the E branch doesn't stop at Hynes.

## Common questions

### Is the Boston Public Library open on Sunday?

Yes. The Central Library at Copley is open from 11 a.m. to 5 p.m. on Sunday, and from 9 a.m. the rest of the week.

### Where can a study group meet after 8 p.m. in Boston?

Trident Booksellers & Cafe is open until 10 p.m. every night. Caffè Nero near Chinatown stays open until 9 p.m. Monday to Saturday.

### Can you reserve a table to study at a Boston cafe?

At two of these picks, yes. Trident and Newsfeed Café take reservations. The others are walk-in, so arrive early to sit together.
