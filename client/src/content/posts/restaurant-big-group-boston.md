A good restaurant for a big group in Boston takes reservations and sits a short walk from a T station that most of the group can reach without a transfer. For most groups, that means the Financial District near State and Aquarium. You have ten or twelve people coming from Cambridge, Quincy and Revere, and somebody has to call a restaurant and give a headcount.

This post lists seven restaurants that seat groups, then covers which station to plan around, how to book a long table, and how everyone gets home. It is part of our guide to [Boston](/blog/boston).

## Which restaurant for a big group in Boston should you book?

Book one that takes reservations and is good for groups, then check its hours for your night. All seven below do both, and each note says which night it suits.

:::places

The hours, price levels, reservations and walk times on these cards come from Google Maps as of October 2026. Walk times start at the MBTA station named in each note.

## Which T station should a big group plan around?

Plan around State or Aquarium unless most of the group lives on one line. State has the Orange and Blue lines, Aquarium has the Blue Line, and Downtown Crossing, for the Red Line, is a few minutes' walk from State. Six of the seven picks are within a 6-minute walk of State or Aquarium. Our [downtown Boston](/blog/boston/downtown) page has more on the area.

![](broad-street-financial-district-rowes-wharf-arch.jpg)

Count the transfers before you pick. Write down each person's home station, then count how many of them would change trains to reach State, Aquarium or Broadway. Choose the station with the fewest changes.

![](christopher-columbus-waterfront-park-aerial-boston.jpg)

When most people ride the Red Line from Dorchester, Quincy or Braintree, skip downtown. Shy Bird, in South Boston, is a short walk from Broadway, two stops south of Downtown Crossing.

![](broadway-station-red-line-platform-south-boston.jpg)

## How do you book a long table for 10 or more?

Call the restaurant, because a table this size often isn't offered online. Give a firm headcount, the date and a backup time.

1. Collect yes or no answers before you call, and add one seat for a late plus-one.
2. Ask whether the group sits at one table or two next to each other.
3. Ask whether there's a set menu, a deposit or a minimum for a group this size.
4. Ask how the check is split, and tell everyone before the night.

## How does everyone get home after dinner?

Watch the clock, because the subway doesn't run all night. The MBTA's [subway guide](https://www.mbta.com/guides/subway-guide) says to reach a downtown transfer station by 12:35 a.m. Sunday to Thursday, or 1:30 a.m. on Friday and Saturday, to make the last connections.

Riders on commuter rail need the last train on the [MBTA's commuter rail schedules](https://www.mbta.com/schedules/commuter-rail), which is often earlier. Seat them near the end of the table so they can leave without breaking up the group.

## Common questions

### Which Boston restaurants take reservations for a big group?

All seven on this list take reservations and are good for groups. For ten or more, call ahead rather than booking online.

### Where can a big group eat downtown on a Sunday?

Elephant & Castle, Mr. Dooley's, State Street Provisions and Joe's Waterfront are open on Sundays. Central Wharf Co. and Lily's are closed that day.

### Is there a restaurant for a big group near the Red Line?

Elephant & Castle is about a 4-minute walk from Downtown Crossing, on the Red and Orange lines. Shy Bird is about a 9-minute walk from Broadway, which is also on the Red Line.
