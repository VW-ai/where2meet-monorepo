A restaurant for a big group in Boston works best when its menu is made for sharing, such as family-style platters, meze, shared pizzas or a grill in the center of the table. Book that kind of restaurant in the neighborhood most of your guests reach on one T line. You're organizing a birthday or a reunion for 12 to 20 people, and they live everywhere from Somerville to Quincy.

This post suggests seven restaurants in seven neighborhoods, then covers why shared plates suit a long table, how to pick the neighborhood, and how to book and pay. It's part of our guide to [Boston](/blog/boston).

## Which restaurant for a big group in Boston should you book?

Book one that serves food meant for sharing and sits within a 10-minute walk of a T stop. Six of the seven below take reservations, and the seventh is a food hall for a night when nobody could book.

## Why do shared plates work for a table of 12 or more?

Shared plates let a long table eat together without twelve separate orders. The kitchen sends a few large dishes at a time, and everyone tries some of each.

- **One person orders.** One or two guests order for the whole table, by headcount.
- **Dietary needs come first.** Collect allergies when people say yes, and pass them to whoever orders.
- **The check splits by head.** Tell everyone before the night that the bill is divided evenly.
- **Ask about the table.** Ask when you book whether the whole group sits at one table or across two next to each other.

## Which neighborhood should a big group choose?

Choose the neighborhood on the T line that the most guests already ride. Write down where everyone starts, count who rides each line, and pick the stop with the fewest transfers.

- **Orange Line.** Chinatown is one stop from Downtown Crossing. Haymarket, for the North End, also has the Green Line's D and E branches.
- **Red Line.** Harvard suits guests coming from Alewife, Davis, Porter or Central.
- **Green Line.** The B branch runs to Harvard Avenue in Allston, and the D branch stops at Fenway.

The MBTA's [subway maps](https://www.mbta.com/schedules/subway) show every stop. Check its [service alerts](https://www.mbta.com/alerts/subway) the day before.

The Seaport and the South End are on the Silver Line. The SL1, SL2 and SL3 run from South Station to Courthouse, which suits guests on commuter rail. The SL4 and SL5 run along Washington Street through the South End.

## How do you book and pay for 15 people?

Call the restaurant, because a table this size often isn't offered online. Have the headcount, the date and a second time ready.

1. Ask whether there's a set menu or a family-style package for groups, and the price per person.
2. Ask about a deposit, a minimum spend or a service charge added for a large party.
3. Confirm the final headcount a day or two before, and ask how late you can change it.
4. Ask for one check, then post each person's share in the group chat.

## Common questions

### Which Boston restaurants serve family style for a big group?

Peach Farm in Chinatown and Myers+Chang in the South End serve dishes family style, and Committee in the Seaport serves meze as small plates. All three take reservations and are good for groups.

### Where can a big group eat late in Boston?

Peach Farm is open until midnight every day. Committee and Korean Garden stay open until 1 a.m. from Thursday to Saturday.

### Can a big group eat in Boston without a reservation?

Yes. Time Out Market in Fenway doesn't take reservations, and each guest orders from one of its kitchens. Arrive early on a Friday or Saturday so the group can find seats close together.
