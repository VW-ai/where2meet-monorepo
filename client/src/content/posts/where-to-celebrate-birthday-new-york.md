Where to celebrate a birthday in New York depends on the trip as much as the venue. Pick a place a few minutes' walk from a station on the lines your guests already ride, then pick the kind of party. Your friends live in Brooklyn, Queens and New Jersey, and the group chat can't settle on one spot.

This post suggests eight places in Manhattan for an evening out, a brunch or a kid's party. Then it covers booking for a group, getting everyone home and planning it together. It is part of our guide to [New York](/blog/new-york).

## Where to celebrate a birthday so every guest can get there?

Pick an area on top of stations that most guests reach without a transfer. Four of these picks are a few blocks from Madison Square Park, where the 6, F, R and W stop along 23rd Street.

:::places

The Flatiron picks also give you a backup. If the first place is full, the next one is a short walk away, and nobody has to take another train.

![](madison-square-park-path-flatiron.jpg)

For guests coming by bus from New Jersey, Midtown is closer. The Port Authority Bus Terminal is at 42nd Street and Eighth Avenue, a few blocks from Times Square. For a dinner-only plan downtown, see [how to pick a restaurant for a group dinner](/blog/new-york/lower-manhattan/how-to-pick-a-restaurant-for-a-group-dinner).

## What kind of birthday plan works for a mixed group?

Choose the plan by who's coming. A game suits guests who don't know each other, a long table suits close friends, and a daytime ticket suits kids.

At a game bar, guests mix between rounds. For a sit-down dinner, ask for one table instead of two pushed together, so the birthday person can hear both ends. For a party of two, our [date night bars in Manhattan](/blog/new-york/date-night-bars-manhattan) work as well.

![](new-world-stages-west-50th-street.jpg)

A daytime show or museum fits a kid's birthday. It ends at a set time, so parents can plan pickups. Buy all the tickets at once, so the group enters together.

## Should you book ahead for a birthday group?

Yes. Book a table or tickets as soon as you know the date and a rough headcount, then confirm the final number with the venue a day or two before.

When you book, ask three things:

- whether a large group needs a deposit or a minimum spend
- whether you can bring a cake, and whether there's a fee to serve it
- how long you can keep the table

## How do guests get home after a late birthday?

Check the venue's closing time and the late-night trains together. Some lines stop late at night, and guests from farther out need the last train they can catch.

Late at night, the R and W don't stop at 23 St or 28 St, and the N and Q stop there instead. The 6, F, A, E, L and 1 run all night. The C stops at about 11 p.m. PATH's 23 St station closes from midnight to 5 a.m., as the [PATH schedules](https://www.panynj.gov/path/en/schedules-maps.html) say, so New Jersey guests walk to 33 St. The MTA's [late-night subway map](https://www.mta.info/map/5336) shows every line that runs overnight.

## Common questions

### Where can I celebrate a birthday with kids in Manhattan?

Pick a daytime place with tickets and a set end time. A museum or a family show on a weekend afternoon works, and both of the family picks here are within a 6-minute walk of a subway station.

### Can I bring my own birthday cake to a restaurant?

Ask the venue when you book. Some allow it and some charge a fee for each guest to cut and serve it, so get the answer before you buy the cake.

### Which area is easiest for guests from Brooklyn, Queens and New Jersey?

Try the Flatiron District around 23rd Street. The F, R and W connect it to Brooklyn and Queens, and PATH trains from Hoboken and Jersey City stop at 23 St on Sixth Avenue.
