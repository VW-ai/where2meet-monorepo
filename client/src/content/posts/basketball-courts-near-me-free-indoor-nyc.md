If you're under 25, the free indoor basketball courts in Manhattan are at NYC Parks recreation centers, where your membership costs nothing. Adults 25 to 61 pay $100 to $150 a year. Your group of four or five wants to run three on three when it's cold or raining, and nobody wants to rent a private gym.

This post lists the six Parks centers in Manhattan with an indoor basketball court. Then it covers what membership costs, how a small group gets in and when the gyms close. It is part of our guide to [New York](/blog/new-york).

## Which Manhattan gyms have free indoor basketball courts?

Six Parks recreation centers in Manhattan list an indoor basketball court, free for members under 25. Pick the one on the line your group already rides.

:::places

The hours and courts on these cards come from each center's NYC Parks page, and the walk times from Google Maps, as of October 2026.

Downtown has only one, the Alfred E. Smith center by the Smith Houses in Two Bridges. For a group in [Lower Manhattan](/blog/new-york/lower-manhattan), it's the closest indoor court.

![](alfred-e-smith-houses-two-bridges.jpg)

Three are in Chelsea and Midtown, as far north as West 60th Street. Washington Heights and Harlem have the other two. NYC Parks also runs [recreation centers in all five boroughs](https://www.nycgovparks.org/facilities/recreationcenters), so a group based in Brooklyn or the Bronx may find one closer.

## Is it really free to play?

For players under 25, yes. Everyone 25 and older needs a paid membership.

The [membership page](https://www.nycgovparks.org/programs/recreation-centers/membership) lists these prices:

- under 18, and 18 through 24, free
- 25 through 61, $150 a year for every center, or $100 for centers without an indoor pool
- 62 and over, people with disabilities and veterans, $25 a year

Adults 25 to 61 can buy six months instead, for $75 or $50. An IDNYC card takes 10 percent off an adult membership. There's no sign-up fee.

## How does a small group get in?

Sign up every player before the first game. Each person brings a registration form and an ID to any center.

The form is on the membership page, and it needs a handwritten signature. Adults bring a government ID. Players under 18 can bring a student ID, a passport or a birth certificate. Adults pay by card, money order or check, because centers don't take cash.

A gym is shared with classes and youth programs. Call the center before you go and ask when the court is open for pickup. Three on three on one half needs six players, and two on two needs four. For outdoor courts that are free for everyone, see [where to play pickup basketball in New York](/blog/new-york/pickup-basketball-new-york).

## When are the gyms closed?

Check the center's NYC Parks page the week of the game. Gyms that serve as polling sites close for voting. Chelsea closes for early voting, and Highbridge and Jackie Robinson close for early voting and Election Day. Each center posts the dates on its page.

![](jackie-robinson-recreation-center-gym-voting.jpg)

Five of the six close on Sundays. Saturday hours end at 3 p.m. or 4 p.m., and Jackie Robinson doesn't open on weekends at all. An after-work game on a weekday gives a group the most choices. ## Common questions

### Are there free indoor basketball courts in NYC for adults?

Not at Parks recreation centers, where adults 25 to 61 pay $100 to $150 a year. Outdoor courts in city parks have no fee, and adults 62 and over pay $25 a year for an indoor membership.

### Does every player need a membership?

Yes. NYC Parks requires a membership from everyone who uses a recreation center, visitors included, so register each player at the front desk before game day.

### What hours are NYC Parks gyms open?

Most of these centers open on weekdays until 6 p.m. or 8 p.m. and on Saturdays until 3 p.m. or 4 p.m. Chelsea is the only one here open on Sundays.
