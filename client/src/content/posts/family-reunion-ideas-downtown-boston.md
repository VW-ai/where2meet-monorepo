The best family reunion ideas in Downtown Boston pair one indoor stop, one free open space and one booked meal, all a few minutes apart on foot. The aquarium, Columbus Park and Faneuil Hall Marketplace sit within about 6 minutes of each other. You're bringing three generations together for a day. The kids need room to move, the grandparents need short walks, and the cousins are riding in on different T lines.

This post suggests seven stops, then covers where to gather, a sample Saturday and what to do when the weather turns. It's part of our guide to [Downtown Boston](/blog/boston/downtown).

## Which family reunion ideas work in Downtown Boston?

Plan around the waterfront between Long Wharf and Faneuil Hall. Two parks, two attractions and three restaurants that take reservations fit in a walk of about 10 minutes end to end.

## Where should relatives gather when they arrive on different lines?

Name one station and one landmark before the day. Four stations sit within a short walk of the picks, and each serves a different mix of lines.

- **[Aquarium](https://www.mbta.com/stops/place-aqucl)** has the Blue Line. Relatives flying in take the shuttle bus from the Logan terminals to Airport station, then ride the Blue Line straight there.
- **[State](https://www.mbta.com/stops/place-state)** has the Orange and Blue lines.
- **[Haymarket](https://www.mbta.com/stops/place-haecl)** has the Orange Line and the Green Line's D and E branches. Commuter rail riders at North Station are one Orange Line stop away.
- **[Government Center](https://www.mbta.com/stops/place-gover)** has the Blue Line and all four Green Line branches.

The MBTA lists all four as accessible. Check its [elevator and escalator alerts](https://www.mbta.com/alerts/access) on the morning of the reunion if a grandparent uses a wheelchair or a stroller is coming.

## What does a reunion day look like for every age?

Start indoors while the youngest are fresh, then slow down outside, and book dinner for early evening. Here is an example Saturday.

1. **9 a.m.** Brunch at Bostonia Public House.
2. **10:30 a.m.** Walk about 7 minutes to the New England Aquarium. Buy tickets on the [aquarium's website](https://www.neaq.org) ahead of time.
3. **1 p.m.** Walk about 4 minutes to Christopher Columbus Waterfront Park, and let the kids play while the adults talk.
4. **3 p.m.** Walk about 3 minutes to Faneuil Hall Marketplace for snacks and a group photo.
5. **5:30 p.m.** Walk about 3 minutes to the Union Oyster House for dinner.

No walk in this plan runs longer than about 7 minutes, so grandparents can rest between stops instead of in a taxi.

## What if the weather turns?

Move the outdoor hours indoors. The aquarium and the market buildings are both under a roof. Legal Sea Foods is about 2 minutes from the aquarium, and Bostonia Public House about 2 minutes from the marketplace.

The two parks are outdoors, so they suit a dry day from spring to fall. On a cold November afternoon, swap the park stop for a longer aquarium visit, and move dinner to Legal Sea Foods next door. ## Common questions

### Which family reunion ideas downtown are free?

Christopher Columbus Waterfront Park and Norman B. Leventhal Park cost nothing and open every day. Walking through Faneuil Hall Marketplace is free too, so the family pays only for food.

### Where can a big family eat together in Downtown Boston?

The Union Oyster House, Legal Sea Foods on Long Wharf and Bostonia Public House all take reservations and are good for groups. Call ahead with your head count, since a large party may need a set time.

### What's the best station for a family reunion downtown?

Aquarium on the Blue Line is the closest to the aquarium, Legal Sea Foods and Columbus Park. Haymarket suits relatives on the Orange or Green Line who are heading for dinner at the Union Oyster House.
