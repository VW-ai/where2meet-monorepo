Where to have a baby shower in Boston depends less on the venue than on the neighborhood, and the best neighborhood is usually the parent-to-be's own. Pick the part of town they can reach without a transfer, then find a room or a brunch table there. Maybe the guest of honor lives in Jamaica Plain, the grandparents drive in from the suburbs, and the friends are spread from Somerville to the Seaport.

This post gives seven picks in six neighborhoods away from downtown. It then covers how to match a neighborhood to the T lines, what to do when most guests drive, and how the guest count decides between a table and a room. It's part of our guide to [Boston](/blog/boston).

## How do you choose where to have a baby shower in Boston?

Start from the parent-to-be's home and pick the nearest neighborhood with a good venue. Late in pregnancy, their trip matters more than anyone else's.

## Which neighborhood fits the guest of honor's T line?

Choose the neighborhood on their line, so the ride has no transfer. Each pick above sits near one line.

- **Green Line D.** Somerville's Union Square is the end of the branch.
- **Red Line.** Harvard serves Cambridge, and South Station serves the Fort Point side of the Seaport.
- **Orange Line.** Community College serves Charlestown, Back Bay serves the South End, and Green Street serves Jamaica Plain.
- **Silver Line.** Courthouse serves the Seaport waterfront, one stop from South Station.

Check the MBTA's [elevator and escalator alerts](https://www.mbta.com/alerts/access) the morning of the shower if the guest of honor needs to skip stairs.

## What if most guests drive in from the suburbs?

Pick a venue with parking you can tell guests about in the invitation. Street parking alone is hard with a car full of gifts.

The Seaport has paid lots and garages. Loring Greenough House has wheelchair-accessible parking, which helps a guest of honor who shouldn't walk far. In Somerville, the venue's own page points to street parking on Somerville Avenue and an accessible spot by the Bow Market entrance. The South End has paid street parking only, so suggest the T or a ride share there.

Relatives on the commuter rail have two easy stops. Forest Hills, on the Needham Line, is also an Orange Line stop for Jamaica Plain. South Station is close to the tea room.

## Should you book a brunch table or a room of your own?

Count the replies. A small shower fits a table, and a large one needs a room you book by date.

1. **Up to about 18 guests.** Woods Hill Pier 4's private dining room seats up to 18. Abigail's needs no booking at all.
2. **Up to 45.** Henrietta's Table's private dining room seats up to 45.
3. **Up to 50 seated.** Loring Greenough House seats up to 50 across two rooms on its main floor.
4. **Up to 110.** Upstairs at Bow's main room holds up to 110.

Metropolis and Monument don't post room sizes, so call with your count before you send invitations.

## Common questions

### What is the best Boston neighborhood for a baby shower?

The one closest to the parent-to-be, as long as it has a venue that fits the guest count. A short trip for them matters more than a central spot for everyone else.

### Where can you have a baby shower in Boston with parking?

The Seaport has paid lots and garages near Woods Hill Pier 4. Loring Greenough House in Jamaica Plain has wheelchair-accessible parking.

### Can you have a baby shower in Boston without booking?

Yes, for a small group that can skip decorations and a gift table. Abigail's Tea Room is the one walk-in pick on this list, and the brunch spots take reservations for the rest.
