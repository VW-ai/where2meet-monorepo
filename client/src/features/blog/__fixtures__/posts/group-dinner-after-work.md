A group dinner after work goes best at a restaurant close to where people finish the day, booked for when the person with the longest trip can arrive. Shortlist two or three places near busy stations, let everyone vote, and book the winner as soon as the vote closes. Maybe your coworkers sit in two offices across town, and someone needs an easy ride home to Brooklyn. One restaurant has to work for all of them on a weeknight.

This guide covers where to hold the dinner, when to book, which restaurant suits a mixed group and how everyone gets home. The examples are in New York.

## Where should a group dinner after work be?

Hold it near the place most people leave from, or near a station on most people's way home. On a weeknight, a short trip matters more than a famous name across town.

List where each person will be when they finish. If most of the group works in one neighborhood, pick a restaurant there so people can walk over. If the group is spread out, look for one next to a station where many lines stop, so more people get a direct ride.

In Lower Manhattan, for example, a restaurant by the Fulton St station, where eight lines stop, lets people in the Financial District walk over. Everyone else can ride the 2, 3, 4, 5, A, C, J or Z.

## What time should you book the table?

Book for the time the last person can arrive, plus some slack for a slow train. A table booked for the minute the first person finishes waits on everyone else.

Ask each person when they can leave and how long their trip takes at that hour. Then work back from the latest arrival. For example, if the last person leaves at 6:30 and has a 25-minute ride, a 7:15 table gives them 20 minutes to spare.

Trips at the end of the workday can take longer than the same trips later in the evening, so check routes for the hour people will actually travel. On the day, check the MTA's [service alerts](https://www.mta.info/alerts) for delays on the lines your group needs.

## Which restaurant suits a mixed group?

Choose food the table can share, or a menu with a wide range. Shared dishes also mean nobody's plate holds up the meal.

Ask about dietary needs when you share the options, and check each menu online before the vote. In Koreatown, miss KOREA BBQ serves Korean barbecue made for sharing.

## How will everyone get home?

Plan the ride home as carefully as the ride there. A restaurant near a big station lets people head off in different directions without a long walk at night.

When half the group lives in another borough, a dinner on their side of town makes the end of the night easier. In Brooklyn, El Zason is a Mexican restaurant on Atlantic Avenue, about a 5-minute walk from the Atlantic Av-Barclays Ctr station. Nine lines stop there, the 2, 3, 4, 5, B, D, N, Q and R.

![](atlantic-avenue-barclays-center-station-entrance.jpg)

Ask who drives and who takes transit, and agree on a rough end time, so everyone can plan the trip home.

## Common questions

### Should a group dinner after work be near the office or near home?

Near the office works when most people leave from the same place. If people work all over town, pick a restaurant near a station that most of them pass on the way home.

### What if one person finishes much later than everyone else?

Book for when they can arrive, or start without them and keep a seat free. Order dishes that can wait.

## A quick checklist

- You know where each person will be when they finish work.
- The restaurant is a short walk from a station where several lines stop.
- The booking works for the person with the longest trip.
- The menu works for everyone's dietary needs.
- Everyone knows how they'll get home.
- Everyone has the address, the time and the name on the booking.
